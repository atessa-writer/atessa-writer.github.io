PLATE I · ABOUT

An Iranian-born researcher, writing between two languages and one long argument.

Nature is a vast graveyard, where every plant and tree is a headstone, crying out the name of a myth from the depths of its being.

Afrooz Areffard writes under the pen name Atessa. Born and educated in Iran, she now lives and works in Leicester, United Kingdom, where she researches comparative mythology independently — reading myths of death, exile, and transformation across cultures that never spoke to one another, looking for the shape they share.

Her first book, Blood and Blossom, grew out of a simple observation repeated in unrelated traditions: a hero dies, and something of him keeps growing. She is currently developing further doctoral-level research that extends this comparative method beyond the book.

PLATE III · THE BOOK

Blood and Blossom

A COMPARATIVE STUDY
Blood and
Blossom
How Ancient Myths Live On in Plants

Twelve narratives, six cultures, one recurring pattern: the hero’s death gives rise to a plant that carries his essence forward, without displacement. From Adonis’s anemone to Siyavash’s fern, Blood and Blossom traces a shape that ancient peoples understood long before it had a name.

Drawing on primary sources from Ovid to the Shahnameh, from the Coffin Texts to the Bundahishn, the book moves between Greek, Phrygian, Egyptian, Persian, West African, and Christian traditions — treating each on its own terms, without privileging any one of them.

PLATE IV · RESEARCH

Beyond the book: an ongoing research interest.

The book’s method — comparing independent traditions at the level of structure, not shared origin — is the starting point for a wider line of research, not its conclusion.

ONGOING INTEREST

Comparative Mythology & World Literature

A continuing interest in reading mythological traditions side by side, without hierarchy — in the spirit of world-literature scholarship — and in what happens to a hero’s identity when it survives inside a non-human form.
